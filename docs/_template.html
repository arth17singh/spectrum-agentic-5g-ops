2

Lifecycle ribbon

panel ribbon, file app/Ribbon.tsx

What it is for

The ribbon is the strip under the top bar on every console screen. It tells you where each of the five planes stands right now, and it is the quickest way to jump between them. The phone approval page (/m/approve) and the presenter view (/presenter) do not show it.

What it shows

Five segments, left to right: Learn, Verify, Release, Operate, Prove. Each has a thin top line in the plane colour, the plane name, a main reading, and a smaller reading on the right. The segment for the screen you are on is tinted stronger and has a short bar along its bottom edge. The right-hand reading turns red when that plane needs attention. A soft white band sweeps from one segment to another when work moves along the lifecycle.

SegmentMain readingRight-hand reading
Learn"Round N/M" while a federated round trains; "Round N queued" when the next round is ready; otherwise the round number and its state."ε max x.xx of T": the highest privacy spend (epsilon) of any operator, against the configured target.
VerifyThe model under evaluation, or the production model "in prod" when idle."evaluating", "gate pass" or "gate blocked" (red); when idle, how many models are benchmarked.
ReleaseCandidate model and its stage: shadow, canary 10%, canary 50% or full 100%. With no candidate: the model now serving."agreement N%" between candidate and production; with no candidate, the fixed text "4 targets, 2 vendors".
Operate"INC-… active" during an incident, otherwise the number of agents and "0 incidents".Time since the incident started (red); after it is resolved, "last MTTR" (mean time to repair); otherwise "network nominal".
Prove"Ledger #N": how many records the signed ledger holds."anchored … ago" since the last Merkle anchor, or "signed"; after a failed check, "chain broken at #N" (red).

How it works

Backend
Five engines: federation, proving, release, swarm, ledger.
Arrives by
GET /api/snapshot when the page connects, then events on the one WebSocket /ws, sent in batches every 100 ms.
Store slices
federation, proving, release, swarm, ledger, plus live.pulses.
Changes when
Any of those engines emits an event: a training step, a gate result, a rollout stage, an incident, a new ledger record.
  1. Engine emits evente.g. eval.gate
  2. WebSocket batchevery 100 ms
  3. Store handlerupdates the slice
  4. Ribbon re-rendersreads five slices

The ribbon computes nothing on the server. It reads the five slices and picks the text for each segment. The white sweep is a "pulse": when one of six event types arrives, the store adds a pulse and the ribbon animates it for 900 ms.

EventPulse movesMeaning
fl.promoteLearn to VerifyA trained checkpoint is sent for evaluation.
eval.gateVerify to ReleaseThe deploy gate decided.
release.stageRelease to OperateThe rollout moved a stage.
incident.resolvedOperate to ProveAn incident closed.
loop.enqueueProve to Learn (wraps around)The incident is queued as private training data.
fl.checkpointWithin LearnA checkpoint was saved and signed, or the model was restored to one.

Controls

ControlWhat it doesWhat changes
Click a segmentOpens that plane's screen.Goes to /federation, /proving, /release, /swarm or /audit. No backend call.
Spotlight (s)With spotlight on, firing beat 1 or 14 lights the ribbon.A thin outline appears around the ribbon. Panels that are not lit fade; the ribbon itself never fades. Browser only, no backend call.

How to read it

Read it left to right as one model's life: trained, tested, shipped, used, proven. Red text on the right means that plane needs attention now.

How to demo it

  1. Press 1 to fire beat 1. Mission Control opens.
  2. Sweep your hand left to right across the five segments.
  3. Mention that each segment is also a link to its screen.

What to say

"This strip is the whole system on one line. A model is trained across five operators, tested, rolled out, used by the agents, and every step is signed into a ledger."

Judge question it answers

"How do the pieces connect?" The pulse shows work passing from one plane to the next, and back to Learn.

Real or simulated

Partly real The ledger count and the "chain broken" check come from the real signed hash chain. Every other reading (training rounds, privacy spend, benchmarks, rollout agreement, incidents) comes from the mock simulation.

Watch out

The pulse lasts under a second and only fires on the six events above. Beat 1 sends none, so do not promise one on cue.

Under the hood
  1. Browser goes to /before the request, so it feels instant
  2. Spotlight setribbon, consortium (only if spotlight is on)
  3. Server records beat 1POST /api/director/beat/1
  4. director.beat eventevery open console follows

Beat 1 has no simulation action. The server only records the beat number and start time, then tells every connected console, so the presenter laptop and the projector stay in step. The 10 Hz simulation keeps running underneath.
